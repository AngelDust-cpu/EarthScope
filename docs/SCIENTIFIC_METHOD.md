# EarthScope scientific method

The app uses NASA POWER Daily Point MERRA-2. For each complete UTC calendar year, it averages 2-meter air temperature (T2M, °C) or sums corrected daily precipitation over one-day intervals (PRECTOTCORR, mm of annual total). It leaves incomplete years blank and does not impute data. [NASA POWER Daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) (checked November 14, 2026).

Each point's ordinary least squares fit uses calendar year and an intercept; at least five complete years are required. The slope is multiplied by ten and reported per decade. The nominal 95% interval and two-sided p-value use Student's t and assume independent residuals with constant variance and approximate normality. Serial dependence can change these results. [Example sensitivity analysis](DEMO-VERIFICATION.md).

For a comparison, take only the years with complete values at both points. Regress the annual differences A − B on calendar year and multiply the slope by ten. With the same years and regression weights, this equals the difference between the two slopes fit to those shared years. The separately displayed slopes can use different sets of complete years. Fewer than five paired years yield no comparison slope.

The comparison slope is descriptive. The app reports no comparison p-value or confidence interval. Comparing the two individual p-values cannot test the difference. Shared climate drivers can correlate the series, and the paired residuals can still be autocorrelated. A future exploratory test could use a HAC covariance estimate on the paired regression and check sensitivity to lag choice and the treatment of missing calendar years. Short records and repeated comparisons still limit inference.

Anomalies subtract each point's own mean over the same complete years in the user-selected baseline. This does not change its slope. A short baseline is not a climate normal. POWER values represent a reanalysis grid, not station measurements, city averages, forecasts, or evidence of causation.

In Detective mode the browser fills fixed Russian templates from these same response fields. The templates use the estimated sign for direction and the exact p-value for the strict threshold p < 0.05. At p = 0.05 or above, the result provides insufficient evidence for a nonzero trend; it does not establish that nothing changed. For a degenerate interval the explanation flags the lack of estimated residual spread instead of using the conventional p-value interpretation. A comparison always describes the signed A − B slope on paired years; a higher signed slope need not be a larger absolute rate of change.

## EarthScope 2.3: annual climate anomalies

For the selected grid point, compute each complete year's mean daily T2M or sum of daily PRECTOTCORR totals. The reference is the arithmetic mean of these annual values over the baseline; every baseline year has equal weight even when it is a leap year. The 1991–2020 preset uses the dates of the [WMO standard reference period](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/wmo-climate-normals) (accessed November 14, 2026). EarthScope computes its own grid reference from POWER; it does not use the WMO station normals. EarthScope adopts a deliberately strict completeness policy: if any baseline year lacks a valid day, suppress the reference and anomalies. Custom periods can extend beyond the displayed years; a short custom reference should not be interpreted as a climate normal. A missing display year remains blank. Departures are differences in °C or mm, not percentages or trend estimates.

## Seasonal and monthly series

The calendar uses fixed Northern Hemisphere labels: DJF (winter), MAM (spring), JJA (summer) and SON (autumn). These month groups do not describe local seasons everywhere. Winter is labeled by the year of January; winter 2020 includes December 2019. The supported POWER UTC coverage starts in January 1981, so winter 1981 cannot be complete. Calendar dates, including February 29, are counted explicitly. Every day of the selected window must be present, with valid values; one missing day excludes the window. Take the arithmetic mean of the valid daily temperature values or sum daily precipitation amounts; do not average three monthly means with equal weight.

Each seasonal or monthly series uses the existing OLS specification independently. It requires at least five complete windows as an exploratory display threshold. Very short fits are especially sensitive to model assumptions. The nominal 95% interval and p-value do not adjust for autocorrelation or repeated tests. Seasonal rankings describe the largest slope magnitude only when fitted windows use the same years; a ranking is not a formal test of differences. [API details](CLIMATE-API.md).

## EarthScope 2.4: threshold events

Days are defined in UTC. A hot day has T2M_MAX strictly above the selected threshold, a cold night uses T2M_MIN strictly below it (daily minimum, not a nighttime-only observation), and heavy precipitation uses PRECTOTCORR strictly above the threshold. POWER reports temperatures in °C and corrected precipitation in mm/day. These are selectable absolute thresholds, not NASA or WMO definitions. They can have very different meanings across climates.

Only years with all 365 or 366 valid days have a count and fraction. Equality with the threshold is a valid non-event. Missing days exclude a year; genuine years with zero events remain in the record. The raw count trend is descriptive. For frequency, fit a line with intercept to 365 × count / valid days; multiply the slope by 10 to report change per decade in event days per 365 days. This keeps leap years comparable. At least five complete years are needed for the descriptive slope.

Inference requires at least 20 consecutive complete years and a nondegenerate fit. The primary interval and two-sided p-value use Bartlett Newey–West HAC at three annual lags with n/(n−2) scaling and approximate t(n−2) reference. Conventional OLS and HAC at one, three and five annual lags show sensitivity to serial dependence and method. Lag-one correlation uses actual adjacent-year residual pairs. The annual analysis does not assume independent daily Bernoulli trials. HAC is approximate, especially near the minimum sample; it does not repair nonlinear trends, breaks, structural changes, or repeated exploration of thresholds. The result is not a forecast. Regional comparisons are descriptive.

Live POWER probes on November 14, 2026: Bishkek (42.870, 74.600) and Reykjavík (64.147, −21.942) returned 13,149 daily values per parameter over 1990–2025, no fill values. The response identifies native resolution and MERRA2/POWER provenance, but does not expose parameter grid spacing. No equal resolution or broader coverage claim is made from these two probes. [POWER Daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) · [HAC reference](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.sandwich_covariance.cov_hac.html) · [Extremes API](EXTREMES-API.md).


## EarthScope 2.5: regional climate trend map

The first map is limited to the rectangle 39-44 deg N, 69-81 deg E (Kyrgyzstan and surrounding territory; it is not an administrative boundary). It queries the official [NASA POWER Monthly/Annual Regional API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/monthly/) using the AG community and MERRA-2. The API permits one parameter per regional request; the rectangle is split into two sequential 6 deg longitude tiles. NASA rejected a 1 deg-wide test with a minimum 2 deg latitude/longitude requirement and rejected a 12 deg longitude span. Requests are rate-spaced, deduplicated, cached by URL and merged only at exactly equal cell centers with matching data.

The live probe on November 14, 2026 returned 209 unique centers from the two tiles: 11 rows at 0.5 deg latitude spacing and 19 columns at 0.625 deg longitude spacing. Both checked variables had complete 1990-2025 values in all 209 cells. Temperature uses the API's annual `YYYY13` T2M mean only when that annual value and all 12 monthly values are valid. Precipitation ignores the annual daily-rate key and calculates `sum(monthly PRECTOTCORR in mm/day * Gregorian days in month)`, requiring all 12 monthly values. The regional product uses LST; the existing Daily Point API uses UTC. They share MERRA-2 attribution but are different temporal products and must not be compared as identical observations. POWER cells are gridded reanalysis estimates, not station observations; the display rectangles are inferred from the returned center spacing and clipped to the demo extent, not NASA-provided cell footprints.

Each cell's annual series is fit against calendar year with OLS and an intercept. A descriptive slope is exposed from at least five complete years. Inferential HAC(3) 95% intervals and two-sided p-values are only exposed for a complete, consecutive requested span of at least 27 years (the full 1990-2025 period qualifies). The Newey-West Bartlett covariance uses a finite-sample `n/(n-2)` scale and a t(`n-2`) reference. HAC lags 1, 3 and 5 are reported as sensitivity checks, not a correction that guarantees valid inference. The model assumes a linear trend; it does not account for breaks, nonlinear change, spatially varying uncertainty, or measurement error.

For each requested variable and period, raw pointwise p-values across the 209 cells are adjusted with Benjamini-Yekutieli at q=0.10, chosen to control FDR under arbitrary dependence. This reduces false discoveries across the map but does not fix temporal model misspecification. Unadjusted p-values and pointwise confidence intervals are not independent confirmations or simultaneous map-wide intervals. q above 0.10 means the test does not provide sufficient evidence for that cell; it does not establish no change. The map makes no causal attribution or forecast. See the [regional API contract](REGIONAL-MAP-API.md).
